# 2027 Global Investment Outlook: The Year to Embrace Risk (Nov 18, 2026)

Resilient growth, healthy earnings and cheaper valuations support the case for taking risk in stocks, but with a strong focus on quality.


- Key Takeaways Morgan Stanley Research forecasts the S&P 500 Index will rise about 15% from mid-November levels to reach 8,700 in 12 months.

- Elevated borrowing costs favor companies with strong balance sheets, durable profit margins and solid cash generation.

- Strategists maintain neutral allocations to core fixed income and corporate credit, while highlighting UK and German government bonds and favoring investment-grade debt.

- The U.S. dollar could strengthen modestly, while oil prices could ease toward $75 a barrel by late 2027. Gold remains the top commodity pick.

Resilient economic growth, healthy corporate earnings and abundant credit should carry into 2027, supported by an intact AI investment cycle. Several major unknowns for investors seem to be in the past.

That combination should favor risk assets in 2027, according to Morgan Stanley Research’s 2027 Global Investment Outlook. The Firm’s strategists recommend a maximum overweight allocation to equities and neutral allocations to core fixed income and corporate credit, while downgrading commodities and cash to underweight.

“The biggest change since our midyear outlook is how much more attractive risk has become,” says Serena Tang, Morgan Stanley’s Chief Cross-Asset Strategist. “Stocks and corporate bonds have cheapened even as expectations for AI investment have risen. And of course, we’re heading into 2027 with more clarity on two big unknowns—the U.S. midterm elections are behind us and markets better understand the Federal Reserve’s reaction function.”

An intact AI infrastructure investment cycle is central to this investment thesis. Morgan Stanley Research maintains its forecast that the total AI hyperscaler and related capital expenditure will be $1.7 trillion in 2027 and $1.8 trillion in 2028. Beyond the scale of spending, investors will increasingly look for evidence that companies are generating revenue and realizing benefits from AI adoption.

However, risks cannot be overlooked. Central banks could raise interest rates even more in response to strong growth and persistent inflation. Conflicts in the Middle East and Ukraine could continue to drive volatility in energy markets, while Europe faces a wave of elections in 2027 and tariff impacts persist.

“Over the last two years, risk assets and the broader economy have proven more resilient than many had expected when faced with these same risks,” Tang says. “We believe they can be resilient again in the face of these unknowns. But policy uncertainty globally could produce very different outcomes across markets. So, timing and diversification will be critical.”

Serena Tang: After a year of energy shocks, sticky inflation and big swings in AI-related stocks, we see 2027 as a year to embrace risk. The AI investment, supporting economic growth and corporate earnings. And that’s why we’re constructive on global equities in the U.S. and beyond.

However, the range of outcomes is wide and bond yields remain elevated. So lean into quality and stay diversified.

Serena Tang, Chief Cross-Asset Strategist

## Taking Risk While Favoring Quality

While investors should be more open to risk assets, they should also favor quality.

“Resilient economic growth lifts most boats, but not all equally: Funding costs remain elevated and borrowing needs at highs,” says Vishwanath Tirupattur, Chief Fixed Income Strategist and Director of Quantitative Research at Morgan Stanley.

High-quality hyperscalers have access to multiple financing channels, but borrowers with high debt levels may struggle to refinance. Within U.S. equities, Morgan Stanley strategists favor companies with strong balance sheets, durable margins and solid cash generation.

The recommendation on U.S. Treasuries is neutral alongside the overweight allocation to equities. Government bonds provided a buffer during the recent technology stock sell-off and remain a useful source of portfolio diversification.

“Embrace risk, but all the more reason to embrace quality too,” Tirupattur says.

## Stocks: AI Spending and Earnings Support the Outlook

Higher AI investment should continue to support economic growth and corporate earnings in the U.S. and globally. Earnings per share are likely to grow more than 10% in 2027 in the U.S., Japan and emerging markets.

Recent sharp declines in global equity markets have also made stocks cheaper, potentially creating an attractive entry point for investors.

“That strengthens the case for global equities, which we favor,” Tang says.

In Morgan Stanley Research’s base case, the S&P 500 reaches 8,700 by the end of 2027, about 15% above levels in mid-November 2026.

“We also see opportunities beyond the U.S. across Europe, Japan and emerging markets, with Japan and emerging markets showing the most upside if things go right,” Tang says.

## Government Bonds: Opportunities in the UK and Germany

Central banks across developed markets are likely to raise interest rates through early 2027 in anticipation of inflation pressures. U.S. Treasury yields should remain elevated in response to Fed tightening.

Morgan Stanley Research economists expect that interest-rate cuts in the U.S., euro area and UK could begin in 2028. Markets, however, anticipate that rates will stay higher for longer. Potentially lower rates could make UK and German government bonds especially attractive.

## Corporate Credit: Quality and Selectivity Matter

After a year of heavy corporate bond issuance, markets now have a clearer understanding of companies’ capital spending plans and borrowing patterns. Credit markets should play an even larger role in funding AI investment in 2027.

Across regions, Morgan Stanley Research favors investment-grade debt over debt rated below investment grade. In the U.S., the strategists prefer loans to high-yield bonds.

## Currencies: Modest Gains for the Dollar

The U.S. dollar could rally modestly, supported by investor confidence in the U.S. economy and interest-rate differences that make U.S. assets more attractive.

The euro is likely to underperform other currencies as political risks rise ahead of the French presidential election in April, with a second round of voting in May if no candidate secures a majority.

## Commodities: Gold Is the Top Pick

Oil prices are likely to remain elevated in the near term as geopolitical uncertainty persists. Prices could eventually decline toward $75 a barrel by the fourth quarter of 2027, but the pace of normalization remains uncertain.

Even though commodities overall are an underweight in the strategists’ allocation, gold is Morgan Stanley Research’s top commodity pick. An easing of Middle East conflicts could lower oil prices and interest-rate expectations, making gold more appealing relative to interest-paying investments like bonds and cash. The metal should also benefit from continued demand from exchange-traded funds and central banks.
